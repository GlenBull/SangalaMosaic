THE BUFFALO VERDICT. The method survives the round animal, but not unchanged, and the reason is not the one we expected: pose, not roundness, broke it. Moses framed the buffalo head-on, and head-on an outline carries almost no species — anatomical masses gave stacked boxes at every depth. The fix is a different tracing instruction: trace the mosaic's tones (light core inside dark rim) and add the two eyes, each a single 8 mm tile and decisive. Three corrections from looking: nested masses have no shear ceiling; shallower is better head-on (8–14 mm beats 22); and depth ordering there is a constraint, not an expressive dimension — wrong order destroys the face, right order barely beats a flat plate. The one defeat is the horns, which extrude as planks and want a slope (roofTris), not more masses. Said plainly: the flat mosaic reads better than every 3D treatment of it — the head-on animal wants more nested masses at less depth, not more depth.
